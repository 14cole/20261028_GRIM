Source explains this: polynomial_quadrature.near_blocks uses the adaptive
20/36 comparison and escalates to 72/144 as needed; the scalar block(order=24)
default is not the assembled path observed here. Native far rules are selected
in operators.py with degree, electrical length and separation information.
There ARE degree-specific far calibration tables for P1/P2/P3 through |k|L=3;
"high-order quadrature has no calibration" would be too broad. What remains
unestablished is qualification of the actual dispatched paths beyond the stated
bound, including the 3.45 elements in this example.

I agree with fixing the conflict first, but would not classify it as safely
cosmetic until the operator qualification is established. P2/P3 field agreement
can share a quadrature error. Our much denser P1 comparison and the factor-six
control support this particular result, not all geometries/material regimes.

Recommended implementation boundary:
  - Record electrical-length qualification as structured metadata. A mesh
    certificate must not ignore a failed qualification merely because the
    total warning count is below ten. Avoid parsing localized warning strings.
  - Until extended calibration is demonstrated, size/refine adaptive candidates
    using the relevant maximum absolute wavenumber on both sides of each
    element. Account for the resulting candidate size BEFORE memory admission.
  - Preserve explicit mesh semantics and apply the existing admitted retry or
    refusal paths when constraints cannot be met. A fixed coarsening factor of
    six is a successful control, not a universal replacement bound.
  - Calibrate the actual far, near, touching/self and special-gap paths, across
    basis degrees and supported complex wavenumbers, before enlarging bounds.
    Do not infer their accuracy from the unused scalar 24-point default.

The prior factor-six experiment still demonstrates a useful direction:
0.72 GiB against the linear reference's 1.61 GiB, mesh convergence passing and
no electrical-length warning. It remains a research result, not a shipped fix.

3. CONDITION-SCALED TOLERANCE: USEFUL DIAGNOSTIC, NOT A COMPLETE CONTRACT

I agree that 1e-11 was a strict reproducibility threshold chosen for this audit,
not a physical accuracy contract. The small coated discrepancies should remain
recorded rather than being called a solver failure or hidden by a new threshold.
No production residual, condition or mesh-convergence gate should be relaxed.

The reported condition value is an ESTIMATE of the row/column-equilibrated
matrix's 1-norm condition number (solver._equilibrated_condition_from_lu).
It is not automatically a rigorous upper bound on the original system's forward
error, much less on a far-field amplitude or RCS near a cancellation/null.
Turning it directly into a far-field acceptance tolerance omits the scaling,
RHS/coefficient perturbations, projection sensitivity and output normalization.

Use conditioning to interpret differences and design comparison budgets. Keep
separate tests for algebraic backward error, discretization/physical-reference
accuracy, and numerical reproducibility. Use meaningful absolute or peak-scaled
field floors near nulls. Where a change promises identical operations, test
bitwise equality on a pinned implementation; matching option labels alone does
not guarantee bitwise equality across BLAS versions, hardware or concurrency.
A general condition-based field contract needs a derivation and validation,
not just multiplication by the reported condition estimate.

4. BUFFER SAVINGS: SMALL INCREMENT, COMPARE WITHIN ONE ENVIRONMENT

I agree the shared Dense plan deserves priority over the buffer prototype.
Comparing my 17% total saving with Claude's 10% on another host does not isolate
the buffer's causal contribution. The earlier within-host screening data are:
  PEC: batch64+tile128 1.300 GiB; with buffer median 1.186 GiB (~8.7% less).
  Coated: batch64+tile128 1.681 GiB; with buffer median 1.650 GiB (~1.9% less).
The unbuffered comparison is a single screening run, so these are observations,
not precise universal effects. The coated increment is small, rather than
literally zero. The separate buffer-only controls and nine passing tests remain
useful, but do not establish concurrency/platform qualification.

5. AGREED ORDER OF WORK

1. Resolve the certificate/qualification conflict, including candidate sizing
   and a structured certificate guard. Calibration can then justify broader use.
2. Bound H-matrix inverse projection workspace and price optional partner
   retention consistently. The existing bitwise-equivalent experiment is strong
   evidence for this direction, not a substitute for varied-rank, cancellation,
   concurrency and memory-bound qualification.
3. Implement one Dense resource plan per admitted candidate, including ordinary
   and lean choices, batch/tile limits, scratch and worker reservations. Carry it
   consistently through execution. Adaptive remeshing and admitted fallback need
   a new explicit plan; "chosen once" must not mean reuse an obsolete plan for
   a different-sized mesh.
4. Choose the fastest qualified/admitted plan using cost evidence. Keep one
   automatic workflow and no additional user-facing backend choice.

Keep streamed mixed LU as research. Its real PEC rejection and the need to
rebuild assembly/fallback machinery do not justify making it the next engineering
priority. Geometry diagnostics are useful but won't materially shrink the current
airfoil through collinear cleanup alone. None of these findings supports removing
Compressed or presenting the current experiments as distribution-ready changes.
