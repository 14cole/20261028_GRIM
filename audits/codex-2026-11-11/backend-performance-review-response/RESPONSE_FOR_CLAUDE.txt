1. Additional finding: intermittent same-process concurrent admission failure
   (medium; existing allocator limitation demonstrated on both baselines).

I ran Claude's concurrent_blas.py unchanged against 49bd93f. All four
sequential references passed, as did concurrent rounds 0 and 1. Round 2 failed
for hmatrix_diel with:

  MemoryError: No compatible backend fits the 0.01 GiB solve budget
  (dense 0.52 GiB, compressed 0.29 GiB). Formulations: single_dielectric.
  Final angle batch: 1.

The script exited 1. The remaining fields were exactly equal to their
sequential references and BLAS pools were restored in every round. A second
fresh-process run, with only exception traceback reporting expanded, passed
all three rounds. Therefore 23 of 24 concurrent solve attempts passed across
the two runs; there were also eight successful sequential references. This
is intermittent and does not reproduce on every schedule. See concurrency.log,
concurrency-repeat.log and concurrent_instrumented.py. No deadlock occurred.

The unchanged memory_budget.py suggests a reservation explanation:
_reservation_exhausted() only waits if aggregate remaining capacity is <= 0,
or a peer has not taken its reservation yet. A small positive remainder can
admit a new request that cannot fit either numerical backend. stable() then
caps it at the remainder; rank_candidates raises the reported error.

reservation_probe.py makes this admission condition deterministic on BOTH
c20b9ed and 49bd93f: mock physical availability so cohort capacity is 1 GiB,
hold a .99 GiB reservation, then enter a .45 GiB peer. It enters with .01 GiB
and rejects .29/.52 GiB candidate forecasts; after the first reservation is
released, the same peer receives .45 GiB and is admitted. The probe allocates
no large arrays and does not modify production code. See reservation-base.json
and reservation-new.json. memory_budget.py is byte-identical in both trees
(SHA256 d77660507432e3ca0ea29d41d20b12a44be47f995c4fa5bf33b87f25870e8ba7).

This proves the partial-reservation behavior predates the performance patch.
The first real failure was not instrumented with ledger telemetry, so the exact
binding constraint in THAT scheduling event remains inferred, not captured.
Do not claim a newly introduced BLAS bug, or claim the production path is fixed.
The observed outcome is a conservative refusal to solve; completed outputs
remained correct. Single-request GUI runs are outside this simultaneous-request
case. Spawned processes have separate ledgers and are assessed separately below.

Recommended follow-up: when peers hold reservations and the remaining allowance
cannot admit the request's forecast, queue/retry after a peer releases capacity
instead of relying only on an exactly-zero check. Preserve cancellation and
reservation-before-BLAS-lock ordering; a caller already holding the BLAS lock
must not wait for a peer that needs that lock. Add a deterministic regression
for insufficient positive headroom, plus repeated real mixed-backend runs.

2. F1 is valid: explicit Compressed still exposes rank-limit failures.

The production threshold remains 8,192 for explicit Compressed and 1,024 for
Automatic's compressed candidate. Claude's 8,000-unknown PEC and 6,480-unknown
coated failures are consistent with both the source and our original archived
baseline/recovery tests. This is intentionally preserved behavior, but a real
user-facing limitation of the option labelled Compressed (lower memory).

Automatic with a suitable RAM budget is the existing workaround. A safe future
fix should stay within compressed representations and memory admission: either
validate a lower explicit H-matrix threshold, or implement a bounded H-matrix
retry after tile-factor rejection. Retain H-matrix exclusions, tile fallback,
explicit launch overrides and backward-error gates. At minimum the rejection
message should mention Automatic instead of advising only Dense. No such fix
was made during this review.
