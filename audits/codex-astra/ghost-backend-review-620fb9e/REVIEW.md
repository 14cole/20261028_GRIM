# GHOST 2-D backend audit response

**Decision.** Retain both backends and the shipped Automatic selection policy. Claude’s cylinder accuracy, mesh certification results and measured timings reproduce. Compressed can save both time and RAM on a mesh that Dense can solve comfortably. The timing crossover depends on the host, thread count and problem. The evidence supports a desktop comparison on representative geometry before changing the default.

I checked the full complex arrays for 48 fixed-mesh solves, 12 Automatic certifications and 46 timing runs. The fresh calculations below used the public solver on a second Linux host. ## Accuracy and what the certificate covers

An independent cylindrical-wave calculation imposed the exterior interface and PEC core conditions directly for each harmonic. Across all 120 polarized arrays in Claude’s accuracy and certification studies, the analytical references agreed within `1.16e-14` of peak amplitude. On the 24 identical-mesh backend pairs the largest complex-field difference was `3.17e-11`. Each doubling of outer polygon sides reduced the worst error by a factor of 3.71–4.50 across the six accuracy fixtures. At 23,936 unknowns both archived backends were within **0.000308%** of the analytical field and within `5.73e-10` of each other. Here and below, peak-relative error means maximum absolute complex-field difference divided by the peak reference magnitude. A translated circle exercises phase versus angle and has constant exact monostatic magnitude.

I reran standard Automatic certification over 360 incident angles for a PEC core of radius 40 mm, outer coating radius 50 mm, at 6 GHz. Both runs passed and published the fine mesh.

| Input sides, outer / inner | Final panels | Reported base-to-fine change | TE error vs ideal circle | TM error vs ideal circle | Largest magnitude discrepancy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 / 12 | 612 | 0.01852% | 111.80% | 40.62% | 6.365 dB |
| 360 / 288 | 972 | 0.00193% | 0.1487% | 0.0466% | 0.00952 dB |

**User guidance.** Certification measures convergence on the straight segments supplied. For a curved part, increase the number of vertices in the source contour, rerun, and compare the fields. Adding solver panels to an existing straight chord preserves that chord. The 111.80% figure measures complex amplitude and includes phase. The GUI’s short PASS message could make its scope explicit with “Mesh convergence passed on the supplied geometry.” The certificate already records that scope. ## Time and RAM

I reran Claude’s unmodified cylinder timing driver on an AMD EPYC 7763 Linux worker with a four-CPU quota, 16 GiB cgroup limit and OpenBLAS 0.3.30. Same 10 GHz coated PEC cylinder, 0.20 m core diameter, 0.24 m outer diameter, relative permittivity 2.8 − 0.06i, 3,872 panels and 5,984 unknowns **per polarization system**. Each job solved both polarizations at 360 distinct incident angles on a single specified mesh. Times are seconds inside the solve wrapper and RAM is fresh-process peak RSS.

| Threads | Dense seconds | Compressed seconds | Dense / Compressed median GiB | Claude's Intel medians in seconds, Dense / Compressed |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 70.9 | 52.1 | 1.901 / 0.692 | 51.5 / 42.5 |
| 2 | 47.2, 40.9 | 35.4, 36.3 | 1.912 / 0.727 | Unmeasured |
| 4 | 26.6 | 30.1 | 1.913 / 0.770 | 22.6 / 31.8 |

The one- and four-thread winner reproduced on the second machine. With two threads, Compressed took **35.9 s versus 44.0 s** by median, and its peak RSS was **62% lower**. Small verification jobs shared the CPU quota during portions of that group. The two-thread ranges do not overlap. All eight jobs passed, the two-thread repeat fields were bit-identical, and the largest cross-host field difference was `4.90e-12` of peak. Timings at one and four threads are single samples here.

**The user's 180-angle sweep.** Claude's 180-angle timing rows used 0°, 2°, …, 358°. I also solved a noncircular coated PEC ellipse from −90° through +89° at one-degree spacing, with the same material, frequency, panel and unknown counts as the cylinder. Its outer dimensions were 0.300 × 0.192 m and its core 0.250 × 0.160 m. At two threads, Dense took 33.98 s and 1.858 GiB, Compressed 31.95 s and 0.658 GiB. The 65% RAM saving was substantial. The 2.03 s difference from one run each does not resolve the faster backend on this fixture. Magnitude varied by 7.27 dB (TE) and 6.96 dB (TM) across the sweep. Both gates passed. Peak-normalized disagreement was `1.31e-10` for TE and `6.13e-11` for TM. After removing the known translation phase, the expected mirror symmetry held within `9.27e-11` across both methods and polarizations. Absolute accuracy on this ellipse still needs an independent field reference or a convergence study.

**Largest archived case.** At four threads, 15,488 panels and 23,936 unknowns per system, Claude measured **171.95 s and 3.059 GiB Compressed** versus **2,959.47 s and 9.259 GiB Dense**. I verified both saved fields, logs and medians. There was one completed run per backend at that size. Compressed used 33% of Dense’s peak RSS and finished 17.2 times as fast. At 11,968 unknowns it used 23% of Dense’s peak. At 2,992 and four threads it saved 29%. A single memory ratio does not describe the sweep. All four sizes refined the same 0.24 m cylinder at 10 GHz, about eight free-space wavelengths across. These runs do not measure the capacity of a 20 ft model on a 32 GB desktop.

**Why Dense stayed below 10 GiB.** I replayed the production planner on the full 23,936-unknown geometry with the recorded 13.602 GiB solver budget. With scratch space available, it selected in-memory double-precision LU, disk storage for 8.537 GiB of original coefficients used by residual checks, 128-wide assembly tiles and sequential polarizations. Forecast peak was 9.969 GiB. Keeping the original coefficients in memory forecast 18.461 GiB. Two fresh 728-unknown Dense solves forced the storage modes and returned identical complex arrays for both polarizations. This is a planner replay. The 49-minute time comes from Claude’s completed solve. Its historical per-factor events and stage timings were omitted, so the fraction of runtime attributable to disk I/O is unknown. The historical ledger also logged 15.113 GiB initially available against Claude’s reported 13.35 GiB container ceiling. This is consistent with the previously documented Linux nested-cgroup detection issue. This run measures actual RSS, but cannot validate container admission limits.

## Corrections and handoff for Claude

1. The 5,883-unknown historical result already used the integrated hierarchical Compressed implementation. Its recorded figures were 35.66 s Dense and 43.21 s Compressed. That study used one polarization, 5 GHz, different material and geometry, condition estimation, and a different timing boundary for exporting arrays. Its result and the latest 5,984-unknown comparison can both stand. [Original review and results](https://github.com/14cole/20261028_GRIM/tree/7a5e9fdabe0e22de1fd74ef01bb4a22f07567733/audits/claude-2026-11-17/hmatrix-production-integration-review)
2. In Claude's timing summarizer, resolving only the first matching basename can reuse a file across two batches. Six names recur. The large pair is stored outside the JSONL batches and omitted by that summarizer. The independent checker joined on batch directory plus run tag and read both large logs explicitly. All 46 archives were accounted for and the reported medians and zero repeat spread stand. The auxiliary replay of the earlier small ellipse has summary statistics in Claude’s folder. Its individual replay arrays are absent there. Rechecking the original Codex arrays gave a shared-angle maximum of `6.58e-15`, versus about `1.28e-13` in Claude’s replay summary. Both are negligible. [Independent checker and output](./CHECKS.zip)
3. Keep Automatic as the default. At the recorded memory budget its planner selected Dense at 2,992 and 5,984 unknowns, then Compressed at 11,968 and 23,936. The 10,000-unknown preference operates among the candidates that pass the memory policy. It is a reasonable starting point. These tests do not establish a universal threshold or measure what happens beyond four threads. [Shipped policy](https://github.com/14cole/20261028_GRIM/blob/ed90b65ff89826274adacae01bcfbafe143e9819/tools/GHOST/ghost_backend/execution/policy.py#L14-L29)
4. **Next independent measurement.** On the intended 32 GB Windows desktop, use a representative mixed PEC/dielectric cross-section with close boundaries and its highest routinely used frequency. Run both backends in fresh processes on the same mesh and both polarizations, first at the normal thread count over −90° through +89°, then over 0° through 359°. Record panels, unknowns per system, effective threads and BLAS, actual backend, factor and residual-storage events, stage and total wall times, scratch use, peak working set and peak private commit. Save raw complex fields. Check one contour/mesh refinement for field stability and alternate backend order on a repeat before treating a small timing lead as actionable. Choose the fixture and record the independent results before comparing with these. Reconcile any discrepancy by polarization, angle, solver path and measured repeat spread. This is the evidence needed for desktop capacity or a change to Automatic.
