# Review of Claude’s GHOST backend reply at f2f08b5

**Decision.** Retain Dense, Compressed and the current Automatic policy. On the measured 10 GHz coated cylinder the faster backend at about 6,000 unknowns changes with thread count and hardware. Compressed’s memory advantage is much more consistent. The new accuracy checks support the previous mesh guidance.

## Independent checks of Claude's results

I verified all 170 files in the supplied audit against their Git blob IDs and all 528 files in my solver export against shipped main at `ed90b65`. Running Claude’s corrected summarizer reproduced the committed JSON byte for byte. My separate checker joined all 60 timing runs to 60 distinct field archives, including all 14 new runs, and compared both polarizations at every angle. The fixture is a 2-D PEC cylinder with a 0.100 m core radius and a 0.120 m outer coating radius, relative permittivity 2.8 − 0.06i and relative permeability 1. All new timing cases use 360 distinct monostatic incident directions and a fixed mesh.

- **Field accuracy.** On the two Xeon hosts, like-for-like repeats agreed bit for bit at the same thread count. Across thread counts, the largest same-backend difference was `2.98e-12` of peak amplitude. The worst Dense–Compressed difference on the second Xeon was `1.73e-10`. Checking the stored fields against an independent analytical cylinder solution gave maximum complex-amplitude errors of **0.00484%** at 5,984 unknowns and **0.00121%** at 11,968. The earlier EPYC arrays also agree with the second Xeon within `5.58e-12` at matched settings.
- **Two threads.** On the second Xeon, Dense finished first in each of three paired comparisons at 5,984 unknowns. Medians were **22.450 s Dense and 24.545 s Compressed**, with overlapping ranges. Dense used **1.864 GiB** against **0.748 GiB** for Compressed. Our EPYC medians from the prior review were **44.034 s Dense and 35.852 s Compressed**. At this size, all three hosts favored Compressed at one thread and Dense at four. Choosing the fastest backend at two threads requires a measurement on the intended machine.
- **Host change.** The second Xeon took 23.3–34.3% less time than the first on the matched jobs. Winners persisted and the speed ratios varied somewhat. The largest change in median peak RSS was **1.42%** (0.708 to 0.698 GiB). The report’s 1% figure is slightly low.
- **Storage.** Claude’s second Xeon returned identical fields for its two forced Dense configurations at 11,968 unknowns. Switching from in-memory residuals to disk increased the solve time from **66.951 to 83.771 s** (+25.1%) and reduced peak process RSS from **6.832 to 2.820 GiB** (−58.7%). Compressed on the same host took **49.060 s and 1.565 GiB**. Each of these figures uses one completed solve. ## What changes when Dense writes original coefficients to disk

With sufficient RAM, Dense can assemble the TE and TM matrices in one traversal and keep the partner matrix until the second polarization needs it. At 11,968 unknowns that matrix alone occupies **2.134 GiB**. The shipped disk plan disables this pairing. An ordinary memory-versus-disk comparison changes both storage and the assembly work. For each configuration the LU factors themselves stay in RAM. [Dense planner](https://github.com/14cole/20261028_GRIM/blob/ed90b65ff89826274adacae01bcfbafe143e9819/tools/GHOST/ghost_backend/execution/dense_memory.py#L125-L159) · [TE/TM assembly](https://github.com/14cole/20261028_GRIM/blob/ed90b65ff89826274adacae01bcfbafe143e9819/tools/GHOST/ghost_backend/twod/assembly/session.py#L49-L67)

I ran the same 7,744-panel, 11,968-unknown fixture four times in separate processes on an AMD EPYC 7763 Linux host with a four-CPU quota and a 16 GiB cgroup limit. Each run used four solver threads, 360 incident angles, both polarizations, degree one, residual checks and a 256-angle batch. The third Dense control used the supported 6 GiB solve budget to suppress the optional partner matrix while keeping the residual coefficients in memory. The recorded plans confirmed the same default assembly tile and batch size in all three Dense cases.

| Backend and measured execution path | Solve time | Peak process RSS | Operator assembly time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dense, coefficients in RAM, paired assembly | 151.19 s | 6.833 GiB | 30.01 s |
| Dense, coefficients in RAM, separate assembly | 168.28 s | 4.930 GiB | 44.61 s |
| Dense, coefficients on disk, separate assembly | 187.76 s | 2.763 GiB | 46.55 s |
| Compressed, hierarchical | 82.40 s | 1.572 GiB | — |

With residuals in RAM, separate assembly ran **17.1 s longer** (11.3%) and used **1.90 GiB less peak RSS**. With assembly held separate, the disk configuration ran **19.5 s longer** (11.6%) and used **2.17 GiB less peak RSS**. From the normal paired Dense plan to disk, total elapsed time rose 24.2% and peak RSS fell 59.6%. These are single runs in the order paired memory, disk, separate memory, then Compressed. They establish the changed execution paths and illustrate the costs on this host. The stage clocks include nested work.

All four jobs passed their quality checks. Our three Dense configurations agreed within `1.01e-14` of peak amplitude and Compressed agreed with Dense within `1.75e-10`. Against the analytical cylinder field, the fresh Compressed result had a worst complex-amplitude error of 0.00121%. The timings include geometry parsing and the public solve call. The scripts and raw fields are in the archive.

**What the disk counter measures.** On our 11,968-unknown disk run, Linux recorded 14.95 GiB of logical process reads, 0.031 GiB of storage-layer reads and 4.27 GiB of storage-layer writes over the solve. Process counters include traffic outside the scratch file. Most reads were served without a storage-device read on this worker. The process peaked at 2.76 GiB RSS. The sampled cgroup, including other processes and file cache, reached 5.37 GiB, with file-backed memory peaking at 2.34 GiB. The same cgroup in the separate-assembly, in-memory control peaked at 5.25 GiB with 0.20 GiB of file-backed memory. Despite the drop in process RSS, total sampled cgroup memory was similar. The file cache can be reclaimed under pressure.

**The historical 49-minute solve remains unattributed.** At 23,936 unknowns the replayed production planner predicts an 8.537 GiB scratch file, separate assembly for each polarization and 128-wide assembly tiles. At 11,968 it used the default tile setting. Claude's 24k log contains peak RSS and elapsed time, but lacks the factor records, stage timings, scratch-device traffic and cgroup cache/reclaim measurements needed to apportion the 49 minutes. The 12k elapsed-time ratio cannot establish a time cost for disk alone at 24k. The recorded 15.11 GiB initially available also exceeds the reported 13.35 GiB container ceiling, consistent with the previously identified nested-cgroup issue. This session’s scratch volume had 6.2 GiB free at the outset, below the 8.537 GiB file required for this particular replay. ## Recommendation for the desktop and handoff

The timing series refines a single 0.24 m coated cylinder at 10 GHz, about eight free-space wavelengths across. At 11,968 unknowns its outer contour has 4,224 panels, about 168 panels per free-space wavelength along the circumference. A physically larger body at the same unknown count can have different compression and timing behavior. Keep the shipped memory-aware 10,000-unknown preference as the starting point. The measurements establish winners at the mesh sizes tested. Applying 5,000 and 12,000 as cutoffs to other geometries or thread counts would require more evidence.

The next run that could change the desktop recommendation is one representative mixed-material geometry on the intended **32 GB Windows desktop**, at its highest routinely used frequency. Compare both backends on the same mesh, with both polarizations, first at −90° through +89°, then at 0° through 359°. Use the workstation’s normal solver thread count. Retain complex fields, wall time, actual solver and storage choices, peak process working set and private commit. Alternate backend order in a repeat if the timings are close. Record hardware and BLAS details so that a changed host or thread setting is visible. The earlier noncircular half-circle case remains directly relevant to the angle request.

For Claude, the new independent check worth reviewing is the same-host comparison with TE/TM pairing held fixed. A further reciprocal rerun of the same cylinders is unlikely to change the user guidance. A representative Windows result or a stage-instrumented 24k trace would add new evidence.
